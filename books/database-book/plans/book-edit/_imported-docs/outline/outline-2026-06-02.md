# Using Data to Drive Business Performance: Databases and Management Information Systems
## Book Outline — June 2, 2026

> Updated from May 21, 2026 outline. Change: moved R.E.A.D. framework from Ch01 to Ch02 learning objectives to match current chapter drafts.

> **Core Instructional Arc:** Data → Tables → Relationships → Queries → Analytics → Decisions

---

## Chapter 1: Introduction to the Textbook
*Orientation to the Book and the Journey from Data to Decision*

This chapter sets the stage for the entire learning journey. The chapter introduces the textbook's philosophy, structure, and the Grading Database project that serves as the running case study throughout. The chapter explains the five core competencies. Students leave with a clear picture of where the course is going and why a systems-thinking approach to databases matters for business careers.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Describe the book's central learning arc from data to decisions
- Identify the five core competencies and connect them to business outcomes
- Explain why this course takes a systems-thinking rather than a tool-first approach

---

## Chapter 2: Foundations of Information Systems
*How Data Drives Business Performance*

This chapter establishes the foundational concepts linking business and information technology. The chapter covers the Input-Process-Output model, the DIKW hierarchy (Data → Information → Knowledge → Wisdom), and the five-component framework of IS (hardware, software, data, people, processes). The chapter explains why strategic alignment between IT and business strategy is a management responsibility, not just a technical one.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Apply the DIKW hierarchy to explain how raw data becomes a business decision
- Describe the five-component framework of information systems
- Explain how MIS and BITM connect organizational strategy to technology investment
- Describe information behavior (Wilson): information need, seeking behavior, and use — mapped to R.E.A.D.

---

## Chapter 3: Understanding Data Fundamentals
*The Foundation of Every Database, Every Query, and Every Decision*

This chapter takes a deep dive into data itself before any database tool is introduced. The chapter covers data classification (qualitative vs. quantitative, structured vs. unstructured), data types, measurement levels, metadata, data quality, governance, the data lifecycle, and ethics. The chapter bridges the gap between "data as buzzword" and "data as a design discipline."

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Classify data by type, structure, and measurement level
- Explain metadata, data quality dimensions, and governance principles
- Describe the data lifecycle and identify risks at each stage

---

## Chapter 4: Introduction to Databases
*From Spreadsheets to Structured Systems*

This chapter introduces databases as the core technology for reliable data management. The chapter explains why spreadsheets are not databases, the classic problems with file-based environments (redundancy, inconsistency, program-data dependence), and the database approach as the solution. The chapter introduces tables, constraints, DBMS platforms (Access, SQLite, Supabase), and the first taste of SQL.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Explain the limitations of spreadsheets and flat-file environments for organizational data
- Describe how a DBMS solves redundancy, inconsistency, and scalability problems
- Identify primary keys, foreign keys, and basic integrity constraints in a table structure

---

## Chapter 5: SQL — The Language of Databases
*How Structured Queries Transform Stored Data into Business Insight*

This chapter introduces SQL as the universal language of databases. The chapter covers DDL, DML, DQL, and TCL; CREATE/INSERT; SELECT/FROM/WHERE/ORDER BY; aliases; NULL handling; date handling; one introductory INNER JOIN; aggregation with GROUP BY/HAVING; calculated columns; and CASE expressions. The chapter uses a simplified Grading Database teaching dataset and emphasizes SQL not as syntax memorization but as a tool for asking precise business questions.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Write SELECT queries with filtering, sorting, and calculated columns
- Create tables, insert records, and verify loaded data with simple SQL statements
- Use GROUP BY and aggregate functions to produce summary reports
- Recognize the basic structure of an INNER JOIN before the full join treatment in Chapter 6

---

## Chapter 6: The Relational Model
*How Connected Tables Replace Redundancy with Structure, Integrity, and Analytical Power*

This chapter introduces the relational model as the theoretical and practical foundation for database design. The chapter explains data anomalies, entity-relationship concepts, primary and foreign keys, relationship types (1:1, 1:N, M:N), and referential integrity. The chapter traces the evolution of the Grading Database through five structural stages, teaches joins as the practical payoff of separated relational tables, and introduces hands-on work in Microsoft Access.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Identify update, insertion, and deletion anomalies in a flat-table design
- Define entities, attributes, and relationships and map them to table structures
- Enforce referential integrity through foreign key constraints
- Use INNER JOIN and LEFT JOIN patterns to reconstruct useful reports from related tables

---

## Chapter 7: Data Normalization
*From Flat Files to Reliable Relational Design*

This chapter covers normalization as the discipline for eliminating data redundancy and design anomalies. The chapter progressively introduces 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF through the lens of functional dependencies, composite keys, partial dependencies, and transitive dependencies. The chapter uses the Grading Database as the hands-on case study throughout. The chapter prepares students to design databases that are both analytically powerful and maintainable.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Identify functional dependencies and explain why they matter for design quality
- Apply 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF rules to diagnose and fix a poorly structured table
- Explain when denormalization might be justified and what trade-offs it introduces

---

## Chapter 8: Midterm Review — Concepts
*Integrated Midterm Review Across Core Database Topics*

This chapter provides a structured checkpoint that revisits and integrates content from Chapters 1-7. The chapter reviews data fundamentals, the relational model, SQL basics, and normalization. The chapter connects first-half concepts to the analytical and design work in the second half of the course. The chapter prepares students for the midterm assessment and orients them toward what is coming next.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Summarize core concepts from the first half of the course in their own words
- Connect data, tables, relationships, and queries into a single coherent mental model
- Identify gaps in their understanding and target focused review before the midterm

---

## Chapter 9: Advanced SQL With the Grading Database
*Advanced SQL Patterns for Deeper Business Analysis*

This chapter takes SQL from basic queries to sophisticated analysis. The chapter covers complex JOINs, nested subqueries, window functions, CASE expressions, CTEs, grade analytics, and performance measurement queries -- all in the context of the Grading Database. The chapter demonstrates how advanced SQL patterns support real-world business intelligence and decision-support scenarios.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Write multi-table queries using subqueries, CTEs, and CASE expressions
- Use aggregate and window functions to rank, compare, and trend data
- Design a query set that answers a specific business question end-to-end

---

## Chapter 10: From Data to Design: Building Reliable Information Systems
*From Requirements to Robust Logical Database Design*

This chapter transitions students from querying existing databases to designing new ones from scratch. The chapter covers requirements analysis, conceptual modeling with ERDs, logical database design, and normalization review as a design discipline. The chapter emphasizes that good design determines what SQL is possible -- poor design limits every query and report built on top of it.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Translate business requirements into a conceptual ERD
- Apply normalization rules during logical database design
- Evaluate a database schema for design quality before implementation

---

## Chapter 11: Database Administration (DBA)
*Administration, Security, and Operational Reliability*

This chapter covers the professional role of the database administrator and the practices that keep organizational data secure, available, and performing well. The chapter includes user management, role-based access control, backup and recovery strategies, performance tuning, compliance requirements, and disaster recovery planning.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Describe the core responsibilities of a DBA and explain why the role matters for business continuity
- Explain backup and recovery strategies and evaluate trade-offs between them
- Identify security risks in a database environment and recommend access control policies

---

## Chapter 12: Business Intelligence and Analytics for Performance Improvement
*Turning Operational Data into Strategic Insight*

This chapter introduces business intelligence as the application of database technology to organizational decision-making. The chapter covers data warehousing concepts, ETL pipelines, star schemas, OLAP, dashboards, KPIs, and the Balanced Scorecard. The chapter shows how the technical skills built in earlier chapters translate directly into tools that managers use to monitor performance, identify trends, and act on evidence.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Describe the difference between operational systems and analytical systems
- Explain ETL, data warehouses, and star schemas in plain business language
- Build a simple dashboard or reporting query that tracks a key business metric

---

## Chapter 13: Advanced Database Techniques
*Advanced Techniques for Performance and Flexibility*

This chapter covers the techniques that separate a working database from an enterprise-grade one. The chapter includes query optimization, indexing strategies, stored procedures, triggers, views for security and abstraction, performance monitoring, and an introduction to replication and distributed databases. The chapter bridges technical depth with practical organizational context.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Explain how indexing improves query performance and when it creates trade-offs
- Write a stored procedure or trigger and explain its business purpose
- Identify performance bottlenecks in a query execution plan

---

## Chapter 14: Power BI — Data Visualization and Business Reporting
*From Data to Dashboard: Communicating Insight to Decision-Makers*

This chapter introduces Microsoft Power BI as the industry-standard tool for turning database output into visual business reports. The chapter covers connecting data sources, building data models, creating calculated columns and measures (DAX), designing interactive dashboards, and presenting findings to a non-technical audience. The chapter ties directly to SQL output and the Grading Database as the source data.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Connect Power BI to a database source and build a basic data model
- Create interactive visuals and dashboards that answer a defined business question
- Design a report layout appropriate for a managerial audience

---

## Chapter 15: Business Strategy and Information Systems
*Aligning Data, Technology, and Competitive Advantage*

This chapter connects the technical and analytical skills built throughout the course to business strategy. The chapter covers competitive advantage through IT, Porter's Five Forces, the value chain, the Resource-Based View, strategic IS alignment, governance, and the risks of weak information strategy. The chapter demonstrates how database design, BI, and analytics are not just technical choices -- they are strategic ones.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Apply Porter's Five Forces and the value chain framework to evaluate IS strategic impact
- Explain the concept of strategic IS alignment and identify misalignment risks
- Describe how BI, data governance, and system design create or erode competitive advantage

---

## Chapter 16: Final Review and Course Integration
*From Data to Decisions: Full-Cycle Review*

This chapter provides a comprehensive capstone review that integrates all fifteen prior chapters into a unified arc from data fundamentals to strategic decision-making. The chapter includes concept integration exercises, a capstone case study, and career preparation content. The chapter reinforces the journey and prepares students for final assessment and professional application.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Trace the full instructional arc from data classification through business strategy
- Apply integrated knowledge to a realistic business scenario
- Identify career pathways where database and IS competencies create professional value

---

## Chapter 17: Designing Systems That Matter
*Conclusion: From Technical Skills to Managerial Judgment*

This chapter synthesizes the entire course and shifts focus from competency to responsibility. The chapter addresses the ethical dimensions of data systems, the manager's role in system design decisions, and the habits of mind required for lifelong learning in a data-driven world. The chapter sends students forward with clarity about what they know, what it means, and how to keep growing.

**After reading this chapter, students will be able to:**
- Articulate how ethical reasoning applies to data collection, storage, and use decisions
- Describe the manager's role in system design, governance, and data stewardship
- Develop a personal learning plan for continued growth in data and IS competency
