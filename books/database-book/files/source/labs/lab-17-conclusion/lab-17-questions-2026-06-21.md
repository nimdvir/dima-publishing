# Lab 17: Course Reflection and PetVax Portfolio

![Lab banner](https://res.cloudinary.com/dkndq6lyz/image/upload/f_auto/q_auto/lab_jpifze?_a=BAMAAAiu0)

*Compile your PetVax work across the full course and write a final reflection connecting technical skills to managerial judgment.*

## Overview

This is the final lab — a reflection and portfolio lab, not a build lab. You will collect your PetVax work from Labs 01–16, organize it into a portfolio that shows your progression from flat tables to strategic analysis, trace the clinic database lifecycle, document three design choices and their organizational impact, identify one limitation and propose an improvement, and write a final reflection on what the course taught you about data systems and decision-making.

- Chapter: Chapter 17 — Conclusion.
- Estimated time: ~60 minutes.
- Tool: Word processor or PDF editor.

## Scenario

You are preparing for a job interview at a company that uses data to drive decisions. The interviewer asks: "Show me a project where you designed, built, queried, and analyzed a real database from start to finish." Your PetVax portfolio is the answer.

## Steps

### Step 1 — Compile your PetVax portfolio

**Do.** Collect representative work from Labs 01–16. Organize it chronologically to show your progression:

- Labs 01–03: Business framing, spreadsheets, data fundamentals
- Labs 04–07: Tables, SQL, relationships, normalization
- Labs 09–10: ERD design, advanced SQL
- Labs 11–13: DBA, BI, hardening
- Labs 14–15: Power BI dashboard, strategic analysis
- Lab 16: Final capstone

Create a table of contents and a one-paragraph introduction explaining what the portfolio shows.

**Check 1.** *(Short answer)* How many labs are represented in your portfolio?

### Step 2 — Trace the clinic database lifecycle

**Do.** Write a short narrative (3–4 paragraphs) tracing how the PetVax database evolved across the course: from flat tables through normalization, from simple SELECTs through advanced CTEs and window functions, from single-user Access to multi-role administration, from operational record-keeping to strategic decision support.

**Check 2.** *(Multiple choice)* Which lab first introduced formal relationships and referential integrity?

- A. Lab 04
- B. Lab 06
- C. Lab 09
- D. Lab 11

### Step 3 — Document three design choices

**Do.** Choose three specific design decisions you made in the PetVax database. For each:

1. State the decision (e.g., "I separated OWNER from PET into two tables")
2. Explain what problem it solved
3. Explain what risks would appear if you had made a different choice
4. Connect the decision to a real organizational consequence for the clinic

**Check 3.** *(Short answer)* Name one design decision you made and the problem it solved.

### Step 4 — Identify a limitation and propose an improvement

**Do.** Identify one thing the current PetVax database cannot do that a future version should. Propose a specific schema change, query, or feature to address it. Explain why this improvement would matter to the clinic.

**Check 4.** *(Short answer)* What limitation did you identify in the current PetVax database?

### Step 5 — Write a final reflection

**Do.** Answer these questions in a few paragraphs each:

- How does database design enable or constrain what an organization can measure and improve?
- Where is the strongest link between a technical decision you made and a business outcome for PetVax?
- Which database skill feels most durable for your future professional work, and why?
- If you were explaining this course to a friend in one sentence, what would you say?

**Check 5.** *(Short answer)* In one sentence, what is the most important thing you learned in this course?
