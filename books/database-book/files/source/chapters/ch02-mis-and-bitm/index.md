# Chapter 2: Foundations of Information Systems

This chapter introduces the core vocabulary and frameworks of information systems — DIKW, R.E.A.D., KPIs, the five-component framework, and the distinction between IS, IT, MIS, and BITM — connecting data to organizational performance and managerial decision-making.

## Chapter Roadmap

| Section                                                                                             | Main Question                                                    | Why It Matters                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Why Foundations Matter](#why-foundations-matter)                                                   | Why do information systems exist before we study databases?      | Answers the "why" before the "how" — data becomes valuable only when people organize, interpret, and act on it.                         |
| [From Data to Business Meaning](#from-data-to-business-meaning)                                     | How does data transform into information, knowledge, and wisdom? | Introduces DIKW and R.E.A.D. frameworks showing how raw records become actionable decisions. Storage alone is not the same as use.      |
| [Business as a Performance System](#business-as-a-performance-system)                               | How do data systems support organizational performance?          | Reframes organizations as value-creation systems — KPIs and the Data-to-Performance Chain make improvement visible and measurable.      |
| [Information Systems as Organizational Engines](#information-systems-as-organizational-engines)     | What is an IS, and how does it differ from IT?                   | Systems include people, processes, and data — not just software. Failures are often governance or process failures, not technical ones. |
| [Managing Information Systems for Business Value](#managing-information-systems-for-business-value) | How do organizations align IS investments with strategy?         | Technology decisions are business decisions — strategic alignment and IT governance ensure systems serve organizational goals.          |
| [Apply the Concepts](#apply-the-concepts)                                                           | How do you test your understanding of Chapter 2?                 | Practice questions that connect the chapter's frameworks to realistic business situations, reinforcing the theory-to-practice link.     |
