## Review Questions

*Use these questions to check your understanding of the core ideas, frameworks, and structures introduced in Chapter 1.*

**1. State the book's central learning arc in order, and briefly describe what happens at each stage.**

**2. What problem does this book try to solve for students who feel that information systems topics are fragmented?**

**3. List the five core competencies introduced in the chapter and explain what each one means.**

**4. What is the difference between the role of the Grading Database and the role of the PetVax Veterinary Hospital Database in this book?**

**5. Describe the repeatable structure of every chapter, naming each section and what it is for.**

**6. Using the chapter's table of tools, identify which tools support data collection, which support modeling and storage, which support querying, and which support reporting and communication.**

**7. Summarize how the book's six parts move from foundations to strategy, and give one example chapter from each part.**

**8. Explain how Bloom's Taxonomy is used in this book, and give one example of an activity at the "Apply" level and one at the "Evaluate" or "Create" level.**

## Reflection Questions

*Use these questions to interpret the chapter's ideas, weigh trade-offs, and connect them to realistic business situations.*

**1. The chapter argues that "data becomes business performance." Choose one business setting (for example, a coffee shop, hospital, or online retailer) and explain how a weak database design could quietly hurt performance there.**

**2. Why does the chapter insist on starting with a business question before writing a SQL query? What can go wrong when students or analysts skip that step?**

**3. The book teaches the same logic twice: first with the Grading Database, then with PetVax. Why is "transfer practice" treated as a higher level of learning than repeating one example, and where do you think students are most likely to struggle in that transfer?**

**4. Compare the value of a spreadsheet and a relational database for the coffee shop example in the chapter. When does the spreadsheet stop being enough, and what specifically does the database give the owner that the spreadsheet cannot?**

**5. The chapter lists several tools (Access, SQL, SQLite, Supabase, Power BI, BigQuery, AI-supported workflows, and others). Why does the chapter argue that the workflow is more important than any single tool, and what risk does an organization take when it treats a tool choice as the strategy?**

**6. The chapter says decisions depend on whether data is captured, structured, retrieved, and interpreted responsibly. Pick one of those four steps and describe a realistic business situation where weakness at that step would lead to a bad decision even if the other three steps were strong.**

**7. Consider the five core competencies. Which one do you think is hardest for a business student to build, and why? Which one do you think employers value most in entry-level analytics, operations, or consulting roles?**

**8. The chapter frames errors and small frustrations as "clues" rather than signs of failure. How does this framing change the way a student should respond when a SQL query returns the wrong result or a table will not import?**

## Personal Reflection Questions

*Use these questions to connect Chapter 1 to your own habits, goals, and professional development.*

**1. When you read the three student concerns at the start of the chapter ("I understand the terms, but not how they connect," etc.), which one feels closest to your own experience with technical material, and why?**

**2. Of the five core competencies, which one is closest to the kind of work you want to do after graduation, and which one feels furthest from your current strengths?**

**3. Look at the list of success habits (curiosity, professionalism, engagement, attention to detail, resilient problem solving). Which habit do you already practice well, and which one will you need to build intentionally this semester?**

**4. The chapter says database work "rewards patience, checking, and revision more than guessing." How does that match or clash with the way you usually approach technical assignments under time pressure?**

**5. Think about a time you saw data used poorly in a job, internship, club, or class (for example, a confusing report, a duplicated record, a metric no one trusted). What would you ask differently about that situation after reading this chapter?**

**6. Which tool in the chapter's ecosystem are you most curious to learn, and which one are you most nervous about? What is one small step you could take this week to reduce the nervousness?**

**7. The chapter frames this book as preparation for the moment when an employer asks you to "pull data, write a query, read a report, or question a number." What would help you feel ready for that moment by the end of the semester?**
